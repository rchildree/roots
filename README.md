# Roots

Roots is a desktop-first Classical Greek verb practice app. Serve `dist/` as static files; the exercises run in the browser and settings stay in local storage.

## Data and scope

The Hansen & Quinn pack currently covers units 2–20, with finite verbs, infinitives, and participles. Its current dataset contains 10,606 analyses of 7,394 spellings for 110 textbook verbs. It is **not complete**: 10 vocabulary verbs have no practice forms yet, some paradigms are partial, and generated forms and unit introductions need scholarly review. New questions are drawn only from the available dataset, not generated on the fly.

Each form may have more than one valid parse. The parser keeps separate analyses and accepts any matching answer available through the selected unit. A learner can identify a form directly or infer its type from a labeled ending or augment; the answer fields adapt to that identification. An imperative is a finite verb with its mood supplied by that selection.

Each observation records where its letters were selected and is checked two ways. Structurally: pieces may not share letters, must come in order (augment, reduplication, stem, marker, thematic vowel, ending), and an ending must reach the end of the word. Against word pieces: where the displayed form has a segmentation (see Word-piece segmentation), a label is right only if its letters are exactly a span that label may cover, and otherwise the app says whether the boundary is off, the letters are a different piece, the piece is elsewhere, or the form has no such piece. More than one span can be right: the personal ending of ἦγον is ν or ον, the future stem of παύσομεν is παύσ or παύ, and movable ν may be counted with an ending but not labeled alone. Easy mode shows these verdicts at once and sets wrong labels aside; Medium and Hard show them after submitting, with the form's pieces. Candidate narrowing matches the kind of piece and its spelling, so reduplication, thematic vowels, and tense and mood markers now narrow. Every form in the pack now has a segmentation; a form added without one is reported as not checked and falls back to matching letters anywhere in the form. The segmentations are **unreviewed**, so a verdict can be wrong where a cut is. Possible analyses and the meter are limited to the included paradigms: one remaining analysis does not prove that an omitted analysis is impossible. These limitations must be resolved before calling the full textbook parser complete.

## Curriculum packs

`dist/curriculum.js` selects the active pack with one import. A pack exports `textbook` with an ID, display title/subtitle, ordered `units` (`id`, `label`), `vocabulary` (`lemma`, `gloss`, introduction `unit`), and `analyses` (forms, features, and their first eligible unit). The reusable parser in `dist/parser.js` does not depend on Hansen & Quinn. See `dist/curricula/hansen-quinn.js` for the current pack; a replacement pack also needs suitable morphology data and reviewed lesson mappings.

## Build data and test

The generated data is checked in for browser use. To regenerate, install Python packages `PyYAML` and `greek-accentuation==1.2.0`, then run `python3 tools/build_paradigms.py`. The importer combines source paradigm fixtures with a limited set of generated thematic, contract, and middle forms whose principal-part anchors match the supplied vocabulary; multi-branch stems are excluded pending review. The aorist third singular is requested with the generator's movable-ν tag, and athematic by-forms of thematic verbs (the later εἴχοσαν for εἶχον) are dropped. Generated records are marked `source: generated`.

Run `node tests/parser.test.mjs`, `node tests/structure.test.mjs`, `node tests/segments.test.mjs`, `node tests/verify.test.mjs`, and `node tests/coverage.test.mjs`. These checks cover ambiguous finite forms, introduction gating, infinitives, participles, and pack consistency; they are not a full linguistic audit.

## Word-piece segmentation

`python3 tools/build_segments.py` (standard library only) cuts each analysis into labeled pieces and writes `dist/segments.js`, keyed by spelling, verb, and tense-voice-mood: for example ἐ · κελεύ · ο · μεν as augment, present stem, thematic vowel, personal ending. A piece that does two jobs carries both roles, as in ἦγον (ἦ is augment and stem vowel), τιμῶμεν (ῶ is stem vowel and thematic vowel), and πέμψω (ψ is stem consonant and future marker). Vowel and ending are cut apart where both are visible (-ο-μεν, -ο-ν, -ε-ς) and kept as one fused piece where they are not (-ω, -εις, -ουσι); movable ν is always a final piece of its own. The conventions are listed at the top of the script. Add `--why` to see examples of what was left out, or `--list` to print every cut.

The generator derivations supply only the boundary between tense stem and ending, and mark contraction; prefixes, augments, reduplication, markers, and the division of endings are decided by the script. **The cuts are unreviewed**, and several are choices between defensible conventions rather than facts.

It covers all 10,606 analyses. 558 of them are forms the generator cannot reproduce (most of εἰμί, εἶμι, οἶδα and ἵημι, the second perfect of ἵστημι, root aorists such as ἔβην and ἔγνων, and consonant-stem perfect middles such as πέπεμμαι); for these the script is given stems by hand in its `MANUAL` table and applies the same checks, and a few are cut outright in `DIRECT`. `--manual` lists them. -μι verbs are cut with present reduplication, the stem vowel in whichever grade it shows, and endings added directly (δί · δω · μι, ἔ · δω · κ · α); the imperfect and imperative singulars formed like contract verbs are cut as contractions (ἐ · δί · δ · ου · ν). In participles -ντ- is the marker: where it has vanished before -ς, the lengthened vowel carries it and -ς is the ending (δι · δ · ού · ς, λύ · σ · α · ς). A few spellings are cut differently by person and have keys ending in a person code (ᾔειν). Present reduplication is marked for the -μι verbs only, not for γί-γνομαι or πί-πτω. `node tests/segments.test.mjs` checks hand-verified cases, that every cut spells its word, that pieces come in the fixed order, and that the curated training pieces never cut where the segmentation does not.

## Shared verb coverage target

`data/dcc-greek-core.csv` is a snapshot downloaded from the DCC Greek Core Vocabulary on 2026-11-06. `python3 tools/build_dcc_verbs.py` extracts entries marked as verbs to `data/dcc-verbs.json`; `node tools/coverage-report.mjs` compares their candidate headwords with the Hansen & Quinn pack and existing analyses. The comparison uses exact Unicode-normalized headwords: it intentionally does not merge aliases, alternative spellings, or inflected headwords without review. DCC frequency ranks need not be unique. An existing analysis does not establish a complete paradigm or a checked segmentation.

The target is the union of the DCC verb entries and each textbook pack's verbs; the DCC list does not itself change the Hansen & Quinn lesson progression or practice pool. The DCC source is Dickinson College Commentaries, Greek Core Vocabulary, https://dcc.dickinson.edu/greek-core-list, licensed CC BY-SA 3.0 Unported, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/. Extracted records preserve original headword and part-of-speech text and frequency rank; full definitions and other fields remain in the source snapshot. Attribution and license also appear in `dist/NOTICE.txt`.

## Construction evidence

`PYTHONPATH=<dependency-directory> python3 tools/extract_construction_details.py` extracts available Greek Inflexion stems, endings, rules, and accent notes into `data/generated-constructions.json`, for generated records and for those fixture records the generator reproduces (`recordSource` says which). Spellings with and without movable ν are both matched. These records are **unreviewed generator evidence**, not verified visible-letter segmentation. The script leaves unmatched records absent. `data/manual_principal_part_stems.yaml` supplies stems the lexicon lacks (for example λύω's perfect and παύω's aorist passive), transcribed from the vocabulary's principal parts; it explains forms already in the pack and generates none. The word-piece builder reads this file; the practice app does not.
